# CreativeDataVisualization
Using my microglia soma size data "Microglia Soma Size CSV.csv", I have created a garden to represent my treatment groups for each brain region and their treatment groups to visualize how the selective ablation of neuronal FXR can attenuate microglial activation. 

My project investigates the role of Farnesoid X receptor (FXR) in the pathogenesis of hepatic encephalopathy (HE). HE is a major complication of liver failure leading to major cognitive deficits in patients that can be irreversible and impact quality of life. With this project, I aimed to see if the bile acid receptor FXR was being activated by bile acids from the failing liver to induce neuroinflammation that could be causing some of these deficits in key brain regions associated with the deficits observed in patients: the cerebellum (motor and cognition), the hippocampus (learning and memory), and the cortex (cognition). 
Microglia, the innate immune cell of the brain, has a major role in driving neuroinflammation. When these cells detect a pro-inflammatory signal, their cell bodies (somas) will get bigger and the processes (branches) will retract so they can move to the site of damage in the brain. To investigate if FXR is associated with driving neuroinflammation, I generated a Crelox recombinase mouse to selectively "knockout" (stop the expression) of the FXR receptors on neurons. This was essentially a "Loss of function" experiment to determine the role of FXR, as the mice with liver failure expressing FXR should have different results than those without FXR if it does have a role in pathogenesis. Upon inducing liver failure in these mice via an intraperitoneal injection of azoxymethane (AOM), microglia in these brain region should show increased soma sizes to reflect neuroinflammation. If my hypothesis is correct that FXR signaling plays a role in driving neuroinflammation via microglia, then the FXRSC (mice without FXR) should show little to no increase in microglia soma size.
